# UX testing kit

A one-hour exercise for the owner to see the site through other people's eyes. Five people are enough
to find most problems.

## Who

- **5 participants, at least 3 who are not developers** (friends, family, recruiters, designers).
- Mixed devices: at least one phone and one laptop.

## How

1. **Send the link and say nothing else.** No "it's a car thing", no instructions.
2. **Watch, or ask them to share their screen.** Don't help, don't explain. Write down what they do.
3. **After 60 seconds, ask three questions,** in this order:
   1. "What is this?"
   2. "What was the most impressive part?"
   3. "Was anything confusing?"
4. **Optional Drive task** (laptop only): "Set the cruise control to 120 km/h." Note the time taken and
   where they looked first.
5. **Thank them.** Don't defend the design; just note what they said.

## Note template (one per participant)

```
Participant:            (P1–P5)   Developer? yes / no   Device:
First 10 s, what they did:
Did the cockpit move before they did anything?   yes / no
"What is this?"
"Most impressive part?"
"Anything confusing?"
Clicked: Take the wheel / How it works / Engineer / scrolled to the landing / none
Drive task: done in ___ s / gave up   First place they looked:
Quotes worth keeping:
```

## Findings → changes

After all five, list each problem with how many people hit it, the change you'll make, and whether a
retest confirmed it.

| Problem | Seen by (of 5) | Change | Retested |
| ------- | -------------- | ------ | -------- |
|         |                |        |          |

Rules of thumb:

- 3 of 5 hit it → fix it now.
- 1 of 5 → note it; fix it if it's cheap.
- "Impressive" answers tell you what to show first in the video and the README.
